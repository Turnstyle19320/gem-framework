What you'll build

By the end of this workshop, you will have a Gem with:

  • A clearly scoped problem and role assignment
  • A grounding knowledge resource (optionally AI-generated)
  • A multi-section prompt with phases, mandates, and guardrails
  • A tested Gem with real conversation data
  • An iteration strategy using bad-output feedback loops
This process -- "meta-prompting" -- asks you to think differently than you're used to. How fast you build your first Gem depends on how quickly you let your brain shift gears. But once you've learned the process, a first draft takes 15-30 minutes. Your progress is saved automatically.
Goal: Draft and test your first Gem prompt. Don't aim for perfection on the first try - you'll iterate your way to great.
Building:

Step 1: Decide the problem + choose your role

Match the AI's role to your own expertise level

Success starts by matching the AI's role to your own expertise. The best choice depends on whether you have a clear destination or need help finding it.

Ask yourself before you prompt
Decision matrix: Ask yourself before you prompt
Question Clerk Colleague Coach
Do I know what I want? Yes Somewhat Not yet
Can I evaluate the output? Yes Somewhat Not easily
Low Friction / Destination Known

The Clerk

For when you know exactly what you need and can easily judge the quality. The AI is your high-speed assistant handling tedious tasks.

  • Transcribing meeting notes into clear summaries.
  • Formatting data into Markdown or CSV.
  • Drafting standard emails or documentation.
Example: "Convert these parent conference notes into a professional follow-up email. Keep it under 200 words, warm but direct."
Moderate Friction / Destination Fuzzy

The Colleague

For when you have a rough idea but need help. You and the AI brainstorm together to iterate on a concept or improve a plan.

  • Suggesting 5 different angles for a project.
  • Expanding a short prompt into a detailed lesson plan.
  • Reviewing a draft for potential missing pieces.
Example: "I need to redesign my unit on fractions for 4th graders. Give me 5 different approaches with pros and cons for each."
High Friction / Growth Focused

The Coach

For learning and reflection. Use this mode to provide productive friction; perfect for helping students or adult learners process new information.

  • Socratic Inquiry: Asking questions that guide a student toward an answer.
  • Reflective Practice: Prompting a user to explain their thinking process.
  • Sparring: Stress-testing a professional strategy with critical pushback.
Example: "I'm planning to drop letter grades and move to standards-based grading. Act as a resistant department chair and challenge my reasoning."
Checklist
What problem am I trying to solve?
Which role would help most? - Clerk / Colleague / Coach
What would "success" look like in 30 days?
Quick tip: If your problem is messy information, think Clerk. If your problem is option-generation and design, think Colleague. If your problem is thinking clarity and decision pressure, think Coach.
How the SBG Rubric Coach did it: The problem was "teachers create rubrics that inadvertently grade behavior instead of mastery, and they don't learn why it matters." The role chosen was Coach -- because the goal was learning and reflection, not just producing a fixed document. See the full example.

Step 2: Identify the resources your Gem will need

Gather or create the knowledge your Gem depends on
Checklist
What does my Gem need to "know" consistently? (if anything)
Do I already have it? (articles, memos, frameworks, policies, excerpts)
If yes: gather key resources and keep them handy
If no: create a resource with "deep research mode" (see Step 1A)
Reality check: Do you need resources? That depends. If you're defining a personality, you probably don't need a resource - you'll include that in the prompt. If you're aligning to researched best practices, or specific policies, you probably do. If you're leveraging commonly known information (a music teacher that needs its bot to be an expert on music theory) you probably don't need a specific resource - simply including "you are an expert on music theory" in the Gem's prompt will be more than enough.
How the SBG Rubric Coach did it: The resource was a deep research document on rubric design, grounding every recommendation in O'Connor, Marzano, Brookhart, and Wiggins & McTighe -- not just whatever Gemini's training data says about rubrics. See the full research doc.

Step 2A: Give your Gem a knowledge backbone

Use deep research to ground your Gem in real expertise, not generic AI output
Make your Prompt
Use AI to help you write the deep research prompt
Start with a new chat. Tell it that its job is to help you create a new Deep Research prompt about your topic. List any authors, ideas, structure, length, the main research question you want answered - give it all the details, and ask it if there are any things or authors' ideas you've left out that it recommends including.
PRO TIP: Tell it to ask you some clarifying questions before it moves on to writing the Deep Research Prompt.
Prompt you can steal (Deep Research Prompt Builder)
I'm building a resource that my future Google Gem will rely on.

Here's what I need this resource to be about:
(insert everything you want it to research, what question
the output needs to answer, how you want it organized,
what people/resources/concepts to focus on)

Before you write the resource, ask me some clarifying questions.

Then write a "Deep Research Resource" (1,500-3,000 words) that:
1) Clearly defines the problem and why it matters
2) Explains key principles in plain language
3) Names and summarizes key research from relevant
   authors/frameworks AND any others I may be missing
4) Is a well-researched, well-cited, complete picture that
   will be used to support the Google Gem I will be building.
Use your Prompt
Once you think AI has everything it needs, have it write your Deep Research Prompt.
Toggle Gemini into Deep Research Mode. Paste in the prompt Gemini gave you to generate your new resource.
Skim / verify / edit the resource (don't assume it's perfect - make sure it's right for your needs!)
Use that resource as the backbone for your Gem prompt in Step 2
Efficiency move: While the deep research is generating, you can start Step 2 (your braindump).
Want to see what a real deep research document looks like? Open the "See what a finished Gem looks like" panel at the top and expand the deep research document. That's what this process produces.

Step 3: Brain dump + Draft the Gem prompt

This is the most important step
This is the most important step. The quality of your Gem is usually determined by how well you explain your intent here.
Aim high. You're not writing a one-paragraph prompt. Think about the structure your Gem needs to be truly useful:
  • Operational phases - Does the Gem need different modes? (e.g., "audit an existing thing" vs. "build a new thing from scratch")
  • Internal rules - Are there silent priorities the Gem should enforce without announcing? (e.g., always check for bias, never skip a diagnostic step)
  • Specific coaching moves - Don't just say "give feedback." Define the exact questions or routines it should use.
  • Guardrails - What should the Gem never do? (e.g., "never give the answer directly - ask a question instead")
  • Interaction style - Should it chunk feedback? Use Socratic questioning? Walk through steps one at a time?
How the SBG Rubric Coach did it: The braindump was written in plain, natural language -- describing the problem, explaining that understanding should never be assumed, that reasoning should always be explained and grounded in the research, that the process should go in a particular order one step at a time (starting with uploading the standard and rubric), and that the Gem should ask questions rather than just give answers. You don't need to write formal protocol language -- describe what you want and let AI help you structure it. See the full prompt.
Checklist
Tell the AI that its job is to help you write a prompt for a Google Gem. Then explain all of the following:
Tell AI who you are (role + context)
Explain the problem you want the Gem to solve
Explain what "good output" looks like (format + tone + audience)
Provide any guardrails, or things the AI should never do. e.g. "never move on to step 2 until the user has confirmed that they are finished with step 1"
Upload the resource(s) the Gem will ultimately have access to
Ask the AI to ask clarifying questions BEFORE drafting the prompt
PRO TIP: Use Shift + Return to create a new line without sending your message to Gemini.

Here's my brain dump:
- Who I am / my role:
- My context:
- The problem I want this Gem to solve (one sentence):
- Why it matters:
- What "good" looks like:
- The outputs I want (formats, examples):
- The tone I want:
- What I DON'T want the Gem to do:

Wait for my responses and confirm when we're ready to draft.

Then draft the first version of the Gem prompt with

until it's ready to test.

Step 4: Test the draft inside a Gem

Put it in Gemini and see how it behaves
Checklist
Paste the draft prompt into a Gem
Run 1-2 realistic tests (not perfect scenarios)
Save 1 example of "good" output and 1 example of "bad" output

How to Create & Test a Gem (Quick Walkthrough)

  1. Open the Gem manager. In Gemini, click Gems in the left sidebar.
  2. Click "New Gem." This opens the editor where you name your Gem and paste your prompt.
  3. Name the Gem and paste your prompt. Give it a name, then paste your full prompt into the Instructions field.
  4. Add resources (if needed). If your Gem depends on documents, click + Knowledge and upload your files. (This is easy to forget!)
  5. Save the Gem. Hit Save in the top right (don't exit without saving).
  6. Start Chat & test. Click Start Chat and run 1-2 realistic test scenarios.

Step 5: If necessary, iterate using "bad output" examples

Feed bad results back into the drafting conversation
Checklist
Paste the "bad output" into your drafting conversation
Say what you didn't like (be specific)
Say what you want instead (tone, structure, outputs, organization...)
Update the prompt in your Gem and re-test

Example: What iteration looks like

First draft (generic)

You are a helpful writing assistant.
Help teachers write better rubrics.
Be encouraging and supportive.
Ask clarifying questions if needed.

After iteration (operational)

Role: Expert SBG Instructional Coach.
Tone: Supportive partner, not a grader.
Use plain language. No jargon.

Internal Mandates (silent priorities):
- Guard against "more work" as Level 4
- Flag hidden behavior in academic criteria
- Eliminate vague qualifiers ("excellent")

Phase 1: Diagnostic Audit
  When given a rubric, scan for traps:
  Trap 1: "More Work" in Level 4.0
  Coaching Move: "Does this require
  deeper thinking, or just more volume?"

Phase 2: Guided Construction
  Step 1: Ask for the standard
  Step 2: Unpack nouns + verbs
  Step 3: Draft performance levels

Phase 3: Coaching Loop
  - One criterion at a time
  - Socratic questions, not directives
  - Never rewrite without diagnosing first

The first draft tells the AI what to be. The iterated version tells it how to think - with phases, diagnostic routines, internal mandates, and specific coaching moves.

Prompt you can steal (Fix bad output)
Here's what my Gem produced:
[PASTE BAD OUTPUT]

What I didn't like:
- [too generic / wrong tone / too long / skipped steps /
   invented info / etc.]

What I want instead:
- [exact behavior]
- [required questions it should ask first]
- [required output format it must follow]

Revise my Gem prompt so the Gem produces what I want.
How the SBG Rubric Coach did it: Early drafts were sometimes overly verbose or used academic language instead of sounding like a peer. Other times the Gem got stuck giving feedback in a loop -- repeating the same step over and over without letting the user move on. These are common issues. The fix was iterating with the AI: naming exactly what went wrong in the output, then asking it to revise the prompt so the Gem handles that situation differently next time.

Quality Check: Is your Gem production-ready?

Rate your Gem against the framework's architecture

A good Gem is more than a long prompt. Use this checklist to see if yours has the architectural features that separate a production system from a toy.

Self-Assessment
Does my Gem have a clearly defined role with specific tone constraints?
Does it have internal quality mandates (silent priorities it enforces)?
Does it have distinct operational phases for different user needs?
Does it define specific coaching moves, not just "give feedback"?
Does it have guardrails for what it should never do?
Is it grounded in a knowledge resource (not just generic AI training data)?
Scoring: If you checked 4 or more, your Gem is likely production-quality. If you checked fewer, revisit Steps 2A and 3 to add depth. The SBG Rubric Coach checks all six -- see why.

What to do next

Your Gem is built. Now make it stick.
Next Steps

Remember: The best Gems are not built in one sitting. They are iterated over time based on real use. The framework you just completed is a cycle, not a checklist -- come back to it.
